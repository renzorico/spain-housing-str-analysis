The 31-night line

In Catalonia, a stay of 31 days or fewer in a tourist flat needs a tourist licence. Longer stays are seasonal lets, under different rules. This page looks at where Airbnb listings sit against that line, and against the other housing rules in force. It reports what the data shows, and what it cannot show. It takes no position on the rules.

Where would you draw the line?

Each bar is the share of active listings asking for that minimum stay. Move the line and see how many listings sit on each side. In Barcelona, they pile up at exactly 31 and 32 nights.

Why 31? A tourist stay is “a continuous period of 31 days or fewer” (Decret 75/2020, art. 221-1). Whether a 31-night booking counts as 31 days or fewer is ambiguous, so 31 is shown as its own boundary value. Other regions draw their lines differently.

Where did December's listings go?

Inside Airbnb keeps the same id for a listing across snapshots, so each active listing from December 2025 can be followed. By September it is in the same band, has switched band, is still listed but has no open dates, or is gone from the file.

Is Barcelona different?

Month-plus listings (31+ nights) left faster than tourist ones in Barcelona. Catalonia extended its rent cap to seasonal lets on 1 January 2026, so the obvious question is whether the law explains it. The test: the same gap in cities where that law does not apply.

Licences on paper, listings online

Barcelona publishes its register of tourist flats (HUT) every quarter. It is the legal count of licences, which Airbnb listings are not. How do the two compare?

One dot per neighbourhood, September 2026. Dots above the diagonal have more active listings than registered flats. Colour shows the share of listings asking 31+ nights, which need no tourist licence.
