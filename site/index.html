The 31-night line

In Catalonia, a tourist flat is let for stays of 31 days or fewer; the rules treat longer stays differently. This page looks at where Airbnb listings' minimum stays sit against that number, and against the other housing rules in force. A minimum stay is a setting a host chooses, not a contract, an actual stay or a legal finding. The page reports what the data shows and what it cannot show.

Where would you draw the line?

Each bar is the share of listings with open dates asking for that minimum stay: blue asks under 31 nights, grey exactly 31, orange over 31. The label marks the Catalan tourist-stay limit. Move the exploration cutoff to see how many listings sit on each side of any number of nights. In Barcelona, they pile up at exactly 31 and 32 nights.

Why 31? A tourist stay is “a continuous period of 31 days or fewer” (Decret 75/2020, art. 221-1). Whether a 31-night booking counts as 31 days or fewer is ambiguous, so listings asking exactly 31 are shown on their own. A minimum stay is what a host asks, not how long guests stay or how a stay is classified in law. Other regions draw their lines differently, so across cities 31+ is only a common measurement threshold.

Where did December's listings go?

Inside Airbnb keeps the same id for a listing across snapshots, so each listing with open dates in December 2025 can be followed. By September it is in the same band, has switched band, is still listed but has no open dates, or is absent from the later scrape (withdrawn, re-listed under a new id, or missed: the file cannot say which).

Is Barcelona different?

Month-plus listings (31+ nights) were more likely than shorter-stay ones to be absent from the later scrape in Barcelona. Catalonia extended its rent limit to seasonal lets on 1 January 2026, so the obvious question is whether the law explains it. The check: the same gap in cities where that law does not apply, with uncertainty that allows for one operator withdrawing many listings at once.

Licences on paper, listings online

Barcelona publishes its register of tourist flats (HUT) every quarter. It is the legal count of licences, which Airbnb listings are not. How do the two compare?

Units differ on purpose: the register counts records, the matching counts licence numbers, and the Airbnb side counts listings. A match shows that a number exists in the register; it does not show that the listing is that flat, and it cannot show how many licensed flats are absent from Airbnb (they may let elsewhere, or not at all). Each snapshot is compared with the register quarter nearest in time; the June snapshot uses Q1 because Q2 is not published.
